# Writing

## Steps

1. **Find the brief.** Who reads it, what they should do after reading it,
   how long it may be, what tone is wanted. When the user gives an example
   of the voice they want, that example is the brief.
2. **Find the shape.** An article: a hook, a promise, three to five points,
   a close. A post: one idea, short paragraphs, a line that sticks.
   An announcement: what changed, who is affected, what to do, when.
3. **Draft.** Write the whole thing before polishing it. Keep the numbers
   and the names exact; a draft is where the order gets fixed.
4. **Cut.** A third of the words are usually not needed. Cut the hedges,
   the throat-clearing and the sentence that repeats the last one.
5. **Polish.** Read it aloud. Fix what sounds wrong. Check the numbers, the
   names and the links one last time.

## Rules

- Say the thing in the first paragraph. Nobody has to guess what the text
  is about.
- Concrete beats abstract: "the export took four minutes" beats "the
  export was slow".
- No invented facts, no invented quotes, no invented numbers. A gap in the
  facts is marked as a gap.
- Keep the voice of the user, not the voice of a manual.
